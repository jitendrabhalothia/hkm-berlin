What is Janmashtami?

Janmashtami — from the Sanskrit janma (appearance) and ashtami (8th day) — is the annual celebration of the appearance of Lord Sri Krishna in this world. According to the Vedic scriptures, Lord Krishna appeared approximately 5,000 years ago, on the 8th day of the waning moon in the month of Bhadrapada, in the city of Mathura, India — in the prison cell of the tyrant King Kamsa, to His devotee-parents Devaki and Vasudeva.

Śrīla Prabhupāda explained that Krishna's appearance is unlike any ordinary birth: "Kṛṣṇa's appearance in this world is not like ordinary birth. He appears by His own internal potency." (BG 4.6 purport). This is why Janmashtami is not merely a birthday celebration — it is a sacred occasion to connect with the Supreme Lord through chanting, prayer, fasting and devotion.

🕛

The sacred moment at midnight — celebrating Krishna's appearance with arati and kirtan.

🍛

Free Prasadam

Free vegetarian sanctified food distributed to all after midnight.

📖

Bhagavatam Reading

Hearing of Krishna's divine pastimes from Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.

🪔

Arati Ceremony

Offering of lamps, incense and flowers to Lord Krishna.

Śrīla Prabhupāda on Janmashtami

Śrīla Prabhupāda, the founder of the Hare Krishna movement and the person who brought Krishna consciousness to the Western world, placed great importance on the observance of Janmashtami. He wrote and spoke extensively about the transcendental significance of Lord Krishna's appearance:

"Kṛṣṇa's birth is not an ordinary birth. He is not forced to take birth due to karma. He takes birth whenever He likes, as confirmed in Bhagavad-gītā: sambhavāmy ātma-māyayā. His birth is by His own internal energy."

— Śrīla Prabhupāda, Kṛṣṇa Book Chapter 3

"On Janmāṣṭamī, the devotees observe a fast throughout the day and spend the night chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, hearing the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa's birth, and worshipping the Deity."

— Śrīla Prabhupāda

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Janmashtami?

Janmashtami is the celebration of Lord Krishna's appearance in this world, observed on the 8th day (ashtami) of the waning moon in the Vedic month of Bhadrapada. Devotees fast, chant the Hare Krishna Maha Mantra and celebrate Krishna's appearance at midnight.

Do I need to be Hindu to attend?

No — everyone is welcome! Śrīla Prabhupāda always emphasised that Krishna consciousness is for all of humanity, regardless of religion, nationality or background.

Is there a dress code?

No strict dress code, but modest clothing is appreciated. Many devotees wear traditional Indian attire — dhoti or sari — as a sign of respect.
